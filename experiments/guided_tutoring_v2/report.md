# Guided Tutoring v2 — experimental report

Status: complete. The preregistered checkpoint grid was selected on dev, then
evaluated once on the untouched test split.

## Why the earlier gain was small

The original sparse-OPD experiment did contact the frozen teacher, but the
teacher supplied only a sparse next-token top-K distribution. Student and
teacher top-1 tokens already agreed at 92.17% of anchors, leaving only 0.409
observed disagreements per rollout on average. The resulting optimization
signal was too small to support a claim that the teacher had corrected the
student's program.

Guided Tutoring v2 instead alternates autonomous student spans with actual
8-token continuations generated by the frozen teacher. Only those inserted
teacher tokens receive cross-entropy loss. Every token group is retained with
its prompt hash, position, token IDs, and SHA-256 provenance.

Model revisions are pinned to Qwen3-4B
`1cfa9a7208912126459214e8b04321603b3df60c` and
Qwen3-Coder-Next-FP8
`da6e2ed27304dd39abadd9c82ef50e8de67bdd4c`. Their shared 151,669-token
base vocabulary passes the explicit token-bridge compatibility check.

## Data quality

MBPP+ was retained as a secondary short-code benchmark but rejected as the
primary interval comparison: typical completions ended before an interval-128
intervention. The primary dataset is the pinned
[BigCodeBench](https://github.com/bigcode-project/bigcodebench)-Long96-Stdlib
subset:

- 142 tasks satisfy the length and standard-library screen;
- 121 canonical solutions pass the locked evaluator;
- 60 train, 21 dev, and 40 untouched test tasks;
- zero task-ID or prompt-hash overlap;
- every probe-inspected task is quarantined into train;
- tests are absent from all trainer and teacher prompt files.

The 21 canonical failures are excluded as evaluator-incompatible, not silently
counted as model failures.

All reported BigCodeBench executions use the locally built
`guided-v2-bcb-executor` image
`sha256:38bd62c7e1e30b684c437329ce5395308aebe7fcd16a772e55d4b21565e6b6de`
with Python 3.11.9, no network, a read-only root filesystem, a non-root user,
and a ten-second per-task timeout.

## Did the teacher actually intervene?

Yes. Across the three five-update engineering smokes:

| Interval | Paired trajectories | Teacher groups | Teacher tokens | Guided pass@1 | Student-only pass@1 | Fixes | Regressions |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 80 | 1,231 | 9,721 | 53.75% | 47.50% | 11 | 6 |
| 32 | 80 | 417 | 3,296 | 47.50% | 38.75% | 13 | 6 |
| 128 | 67 | 82 | 640 | 35.82% | 35.82% | 4 | 4 |

All teacher requests and all emitted groups in these runs succeeded; rollout
policy staleness was zero. These are engineering trajectories, not held-out
checkpoint results, and task IDs can recur across updates. Nominal per-record
paired p-values would be 0.332, 0.167, and 1.0, but the repeated tasks violate
the independence assumption, so those values are descriptive only and are not
used for inference.

## Causal teacher-following control

Twenty exact-trajectory counterfactual trials started the matched and shuffled
updates from the same model, optimizer state, and generated trajectories. The
matched update improved true teacher-token NLL more than the shuffled-label
update in 20/20 trials:

- matched mean NLL delta: -0.04180;
- shuffled mean true-teacher NLL delta: -0.02603;
- matched advantage: 0.01577;
- paired bootstrap 95% CI: [0.01121, 0.02071];
- exact two-sided sign-test p: 1.91e-6.

The shuffled update still reduced its own random-label training loss in 20/20
trials and produced 13.0 times the matched gradient norm. This rules out the
explanation that any large gradient or extra token target would have produced
the same teacher-following result. The trials advance sequentially from the
matched branch, so the confidence interval and sign-test p-value are
trial-level diagnostics rather than a claim of 20 independently trained
models.

## Teacher headroom

Before checkpoint selection, the frozen baselines were evaluated on the
21-task dev set:

- base Qwen3-4B: 8/21, 38.10%;
- frozen Qwen3-Coder-Next-FP8 teacher: 11/21, 52.38%;
- difference: +14.29 percentage points.

At task level the teacher fixes four base failures but regresses on one base
success; seven tasks pass for both and nine fail for both. The teacher
therefore has useful headroom but is not an oracle.

## Scientific training audit

All nine runs pass the frozen telemetry criteria. Across 180/180 optimizer
updates, post-update NLL on the actual inserted teacher tokens is lower than
pre-update NLL. Teacher request success is 100%, emitted-group nonempty rate is
100%, circuit breakers never open, and rollout policy staleness is zero.

| Interval | Seeds | Teacher tokens | Groups | Mean intervention coverage | Mean NLL delta | Allocated GPU-hours |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 3 | 135,873 | 17,167 | 100.00% | -0.02874 | 7.21 |
| 32 | 3 | 49,031 | 6,216 | 100.00% | -0.03708 | 6.55 |
| 128 | 3 | 9,524 | 1,214 | 93.13% | -0.04435 | 5.77 |

The interval-128 coverage gap is caused by student completions that terminate
before the first intervention, not by failed teacher requests.

## Deterministic functional intervention

On 20 train-only tasks, greedy student-only decoding passes 9/20. Inserting
the teacher's real eight-token groups into the exact same deterministic
decoding process gives:

| Interval | Guided passes | Fixes | Regressions | Teacher tokens |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 9/20 | 2 | 2 | 2,501 |
| 32 | 11/20 | 2 | 0 | 836 |
| 128 | 8/20 | 0 | 1 | 187 |

Thus the teacher can causally repair executable trajectories, especially at
interval 32. More frequent intervention is not automatically better: an
eight-token continuation can also steer a partially written program onto an
incompatible path.

## Why interval 32 has the largest observed gain

The test delta can be decomposed exactly over the 3 seeds and 40 tasks. If
`F_k` is the number of base failures fixed at interval `k` and `R_k` is the
number of base successes regressed, then

```text
delta_k = (F_k - R_k) / (3 * 40).
```

| Interval | Fixes / 120 | Regressions / 120 | Net | Delta vs base | Mean completion tokens | Max-length / 120 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 6 | 7 | -1 | -0.83 pp | 288.29 | 7 |
| 32 | 5 | 1 | +4 | +3.33 pp | 214.85 | 0 |
| 128 | 3 | 3 | 0 | 0.00 pp | 252.40 | 3 |

Interval 32 therefore wins mainly by preserving existing capabilities, not
by producing the most fixes: interval 8 actually has one more fix, but six
more regressions. The observed interval-32 advantage over interval 8 is
`(4 - (-1)) / 120 = 4.17 pp`; its advantage over interval 128 is
`(4 - 0) / 120 = 3.33 pp`.

The intervention density in the selected checkpoints explains the direction
of the result:

| Interval | Ideal teacher share `8/(k+8)` | Observed teacher share | Groups / guided rollout | Intervention coverage | Mean selected updates |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 50.00% | 49.35% | 17.42 | 100.00% | 16.67 |
| 32 | 20.00% | 18.46% | 5.48 | 100.00% | 5.00 |
| 128 | 5.88% | 4.32% | 1.35 | 92.22% | 18.33 |

At interval 8, about half of the mixed trajectory is teacher-generated and
there are roughly 17 continuation seams per rollout. The selected models
produce much longer completions and seven truncation failures, consistent
with over-intervention and trajectory stitching causing collateral changes.
At interval 128, the tutor supplies only about 1.35 groups per rollout and
does not intervene at all in some early-terminating trajectories, so the
correction signal is sparse. Interval 32 lies between them: about five or six
teacher groups cover every guided rollout while leaving roughly four fifths
of the trajectory autonomous.

The deterministic train-only probe supports this mechanism without involving
checkpoint selection: interval 32 changes 9/20 student-only passes to 11/20
through 2 fixes and 0 regressions; interval 8 has 2 fixes and 2 regressions,
and interval 128 has 0 fixes and 1 regression. The selected interval-32
teacher targets also have the highest weighted pre-update NLL (0.8733) and
the largest absolute NLL reduction (-0.06145). This says those local targets
were particularly informative to the student, although NLL values across
intervals are not strictly comparable because they score different token
positions.

This is a plausible sweet spot, not a statistically established optimum. The
task-cluster bootstrap 95% interval for interval 32 versus base is
[-1.67, +10.00] pp; for interval 32 versus interval 8 it is
[-4.17, +11.67] pp. Both include zero. Moreover, all interval-32 models were
selected at step 5, while the selected interval-8 and interval-128 models
average 16.67 and 18.33 updates. At the matched step-5 dev checkpoint,
interval 32 is still best (36.51% versus 31.75% and 30.16%), but the untouched
test did not evaluate all matched checkpoints. The positive test events are
also concentrated on two tasks, the frozen teacher itself fails both of those
test tasks, and seeds 0 and 1 have identical pass/fail vectors. The gains can
therefore reflect transferred local patterns learned on train, but not direct
copying of successful held-out teacher solutions. The correct conclusion is
that the logs support a moderate-intervention mechanism, while a larger
matched-update experiment is needed to distinguish a real interval effect
from task and selection noise.

## Scientific outcome

The frozen teacher really did correct the student, and the student really did
learn the supplied token targets. The earlier approximately one-point result
was largely a limitation of the old sparse top-K target, not a failed teacher
service. However, local teacher following does not reliably become autonomous
program quality under this small pilot budget.

Interval 32 is the best observed tradeoff: deterministic guided execution
improves by 10 points on the train-only probe, and selected autonomous
checkpoints improve by 3.33 points on average on untouched test. That is larger
than offline SFT's zero-point change, but it is not yet strong enough to claim
a reliable quality improvement. Interval 8 over-intervenes and interval 128
under-intervenes. A next experiment should test diagnostic or edit-aware
teacher feedback near interval 32, rather than simply increasing the number
of continuation tokens.
